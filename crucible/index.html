The Crucible: Drama Production

Compare Characters - my own POV

Posted on By: Lilly | posted on 11/29 | Category: Drama club production/Compare of Characters
All Posts

This story contains many layers than it might seem. This story, set back with Salem witch trials of 1692, written by the author Arthur Miller’s 1953.

Death. Affair. Witches. And unspoken confessions. The intensity and the crescendo of laughter in the beginning of the story, foreshadowing the tension and chaos that is soon to be among them and will grow. As secrets day by day, minute by minute, second by second are revealed. Many turns will be taken, and many relationships of what was once trust, may be at stake. Keeping the audience on the edge of their chairs, not sure what’s real or the truth anymore. Truth behind characters that may or not be spoken. What goes on backstage?

Something to think about…how may this play be a hit at today’s government?

Of modern governance by showing how paranoia, overrating power importance, and the abuse of institutional power can corrupt a society. The play's themes show several political dynamics.

This story shows the flaws in human nature and society. Showing the timeless mistakes humans make, for those who forget the past are doomed to repeat it. Loosely based on true, historical events, but heavily fictionalized drama.

Containing witches, a neighbourhood who was full of trust, now turning on themselves, a love affair, and much more within itself.

Comparing intentions and endings (Warning: SPOILERS)

Character/role Stance (where they stand) Turning Point Final Belief or Action
John Proctor Stands as an independent complex though tragic hero. Faced with the guilt of a love affair and disagreements with Salem's corrupted Court. In act 3 he publicly confesses his love affair with Abigail Williams in the court, exposing Abigail to save his wife Elizabeth. A turn takes place in act 4 when he tears up his signed confession. In John Proctor's final action is to rip up his false confession of Witchcraft in choosing execution by hanging rather than have good on his name for life. Choosing to die in truth rather than live a lie.
Mary Warren Mary Warren is a timid naive 17-Year-Old girl who works as a servant for John and Elizabeth Proctor Originally Mary WarrenTries to tell the court the truth but with Abigail's intimidating he's in the fusion of John Proctor Witchcraft to save herself. She cracks Under Pressure. She knew deep down that there's no real witchcraft but the fear of Abigail and the court Has her abandoned the truth entirely.
Abigail Williams Abigail Williams is a 17-year-old orphan living with her uncle trying to hide her affair with John Proctor and avoiding punishment for dancing in an attempt to summon spirits in the forest with other girls. In act 1 she admits to trying to summon demons and thanks for forgiveness and praise from the adults to have God forgive her. Sometime later she accuses John Proctor Witchcraft of trying to save herself. In her final action she robs her uncle Parris of his life savings to escape and leaves John Proctor to face his own execution.

My own thoughts on drama/story/production

Being in this production, even being in a small role and learning to play a character reminds me how much worse it could be if we haven’t changed over time. My character is a little girl who is frightened so much that she falls into a shocked stage, causing her to be asleep or unconscious most of the time in the play, or said it was the devils work. Her father, Samuel Parris doesn’t care much about anything else but his reputation, mother dead that she tends to yell out for when she wakes up suddenly for the first time, and her cousin being the main witch and antagonist, threatening Betty to keep quiet when she came to the realisation of what truly happened.

Being behind the scenes of this production and getting to know the story - even without knowing the full extent or how complex it may be. For me, I can see what mistakes humans are still making even now with judgement without knowing the full story. Meeting people behind their character is the joy of having your own inside jokes, contrasting to what is shown on stage.

Seeing behind the scenes is not just watching an art work being created. But being able to create your art with not just decoration or an item but a face and act you put on that stage. The art of seeing underneath layers of not just the story, or the characters themself. But the people behind it made this possible.
